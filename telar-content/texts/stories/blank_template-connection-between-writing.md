---
title: "Connection between writing & speech"
---

In the Florentine Codex, there are three forms of communication involving Nahuatl alphabetic text, Spanish translation, and the imagery painted in the book. Overall, the combination of these forms demonstrates how spoken language and knowledge can be presented and preserved through writing. For example, in the image provided, the little bubbles or blobs in the air in front of the people's mouths are called speech glyphs. It provides a visual representation of the act of speaking. Creating a bridge between speech and writing throughout the Florentine Codex. 

In Jeanette Favrot Peterson's article, she discusses "speech of the elders" (huehuetlatolli) in Book 6. Showcasing the visual analysis of oral discourse that was communicated on important topics such as traditions, ideas, knowledge, etc. She argues that oral communication could become part of a lasting record in writing, not only needing to be physically recorded. The imagery recording these speeches, surrounded by the text, provides strong evidence of how information that was once spoken could be preserved in writing that could be passed down over time.
